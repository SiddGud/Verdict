    For fans

    Your day, and your case

    Name up to five artists you love; they need not play here. Every act on the bill is scored for you and your best path through the day is planned. Any clash you cannot avoid is filed with the court and weighs the next ruling.

    How it works

    Who is in the room

    Code builds the timetable; the agents argue, object and explain. Nothing an agent says can move a set that breaks a rule.

    1. The clerk reads the bill

      Every act is matched to Qloo by exact name. Qloo scores how much each pair's fans are the same people, for the whole bill.

    2. Fan agents file cases

      The worst clashes become cases. Each side's agent argues with its numbers: where the other act ranks for its fans, and how long the sets overlap.

    3. The booker objects

      The booker agent tries the quickest fix and objects when it breaks a rule: headliners close, no act moves more than two hours, set lengths and stage sizes stay.

    4. The judge rules

      An optimiser searches thousands of swaps for the timetable with the least clash that keeps every rule, then each ruling says where the two acts ended up.

    5. Emergencies

      Type "Tyla just cancelled" or "Kesha arrives at 4pm". The booker reads it into a checked rule and the court sits again.

    6. Fans weigh in

      A fan's route flags the clashes they cannot avoid. Each report adds weight to that pair the next time the court sits.

    How we know

    Tested before we built on it

    Test 28 was written down and committed before any result: 10 bills of 30 real artists, judged on how many listeners each pair truly shares (Last.fm). Higher is better.
